# After Graduation

Eight lanes. Generate candidates across at least four of them before narrowing — graduates self-restrict to one or two lanes and that is where the bad decisions come from.

All exam dates, eligibility rules, fees and salary figures must be verified live — see `volatile-data.md`.

## 1. Work now

The strongest option for more graduates than admit it, because it is the only lane that starts compounding immediately — income, savings, and a track record that every other lane later asks for.

- Campus placement, off-campus hiring, referrals, and the walk-in and mass-hiring channels for service companies
- Role families by degree: software and IT services, analytics, BPO/KPO, banking operations, sales, supply chain, insurance, teaching, content, healthcare administration
- Apprenticeships under NAPS, and graduate trainee programmes
- Contract-to-hire, which is a legitimate entry route when the direct route is blocked

Say plainly when a first job is worth taking even if it is not the target job. Two years of employment plus a part-time credential often beats two years of unemployed preparation — including on the metric the student actually cares about, which is where they are at 25.

## 2. Higher study in India

- **M.Tech / MS** — GATE. Also the gateway to PSU recruitment, which many candidates do not realise they are simultaneously competing for.
- **MBA** — CAT, XAT, NMAT, SNAP, CMAT, MAT, and state CETs. Two things to state honestly: the return depends heavily on the tier of the school, and most good schools weight work experience, so applying straight out of college may be a strictly worse version of applying in two years.
- **MSc** — IIT JAM, CUET-PG, university tests
- **MCA** — NIMCET and state routes
- **MA / MSW / MJMC** — CUET-PG, TISS, university admissions
- **LLM** — CLAT PG, AILET PG
- **M.Des** — CEED

## 3. Study abroad

Handle the money first. This is where families are most likely to be quietly ruined by optimistic advice.

- Route: entrance requirements (GRE where applicable, IELTS/TOEFL/PTE/Duolingo), transcripts, SOP, letters of recommendation, application cycles roughly a year ahead of intake
- Destinations differ substantially on total cost, post-study work rights and immigration policy — and those policies change every year, so verify rather than recall
- Funding: assistantships, scholarships, and education loans. Model the loan repayment against realistic — not advertised — starting salaries, and against the possibility of not getting a job in that country
- Common mistake: choosing the programme by country brand instead of by the specific department, funding, and post-study work pathway

## 4. Government and public sector

Long odds, high security, and a real opportunity cost that is routinely hidden. Quote the applicant-to-vacancy scale for anything in this lane.

- **UPSC CSE** and state PSCs — multi-year commitment, age and attempt limits, very low success rate. Valid for the genuinely committed. Requires an explicit exit date agreed in advance.
- **SSC** — CGL, CHSL, MTS
- **Banking** — IBPS PO/Clerk, SBI PO/Clerk, RBI Grade B
- **Railways** — RRB NTPC and technical cadres
- **PSUs** — largely through GATE for technical roles
- **Defence** — CDS, AFCAT, and technical entries
- **Teaching** — CTET, state TETs, UGC NET/SET
- **Insurance and others** — LIC AAO, NABARD, SEBI, FCI

The rule for this lane: preparation must run on a stated clock with a defined number of attempts, and ideally alongside employment or a completed backup qualification. Open-ended preparation is the single most costly pattern in Indian career decisions.

## 5. Professional certifications

- **CA / CS / CMA** — can be pursued after graduation via the direct-entry route
- **CFA, FRM** — finance
- **ACCA, CPA** — international accounting, strong demand in Indian GCCs
- **Actuarial (IAI/IFoA)** — long, hard, very high ceiling
- **Cloud, security and data certifications** — AWS, Azure, GCP, CISSP, and equivalents. These pair with employment rather than replacing it.

## 6. Research and academia

- CSIR-UGC NET / UGC NET for JRF and lectureship
- Institutional PhD entries: IITs, IISc, IISERs, TIFR, and university programmes
- Fellowships: DST INSPIRE, PMRF, and institute-specific funding
- Overseas PhDs are typically funded, and are a materially different financial proposition from a self-funded overseas master's — flag this, because many students do not know it

## 7. Building something

- Startup, family business, freelancing, consulting, creator and content work
- Incubators, state startup policies, and university incubation cells
- The honest framing: no floor, no ceiling, no salary while you learn. Best attempted with either savings, low fixed costs, or a parallel income — not as an escape from an unsuccessful job search

## 8. Skill pivot

For graduates whose degree does not map to a job they want.

- Software and data, product management, design, digital marketing, sales, cloud and DevOps, cybersecurity
- Route in: self-study plus a portfolio, or a structured programme
- Be specific about how long a pivot actually takes to reach employability — usually longer than programme marketing suggests, and it depends far more on projects and evidence of work than on the certificate

## The default question: job or higher study?

Push toward **work first** when: the degree is employable now, money is tight, the student is unclear about specialisation, or the target programme values experience (MBA in particular).

Push toward **study first** when: the target role has a hard credential requirement (medicine, law practice, clinical psychology, academia), the funding is already secured, or the student has a specific research direction rather than a general wish to postpone a decision.

The tell-tale signal to name out loud: higher study chosen because the job search is going badly is a very expensive way to postpone the job search by two years. Say it kindly, but say it.
